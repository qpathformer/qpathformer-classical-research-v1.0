# Research guide

Eleven small model recipes compare flexible circuit readouts with neural-network and trigonometric controls on fresh two-dimensional circle and ellipse tasks. Learned scalar and five-moment readouts improve on the legacy output, while strong trigonometric controls retain the most attractive accuracy and cost combination. The random frozen circuit core performs substantially worse. The study completed 66 development and 165 confirmation endpoints, in addition to 22 engineering endpoints.

## Models and tasks

The registered methods are q2_kmeans, qscalar_kmeans, qmulti_kmeans, qfrozen_kmeans, mlp4relu_kmeans, mlp8relu_kmeans, mlp4tanh_kmeans, mlp8tanh_kmeans, trig_kmeans, trig_global and mlp_global. The legacy probability link differs from the native-logit readouts. Five readout moments are X0, X1, Z0, Z1 and Z0Z1. Training the circuit core is contrasted with a frozen random core. Circle and ellipse populations remain separate.

## Interpretation

The results concern small two-dimensional synthetic classifiers on classical CPU hardware. Five paired seeds and one split per family do not establish population-wide or real-world generalization. Historical prior-version scores are context, not paired evidence of improvement. Local compilation, noise and shot results are simulator evidence, with no physical-QPU result or quantum-advantage claim. The full proposed language architecture was not benchmarked.
