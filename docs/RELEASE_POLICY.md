# Research release policy

A release is an owner-approved snapshot of identified papers and supporting materials. The scientific paper, research approach and repository rules are distinct documents. The [research approach](RESEARCH_APPROACH.md) must be linked in the release notes and included under release docs; it is excluded from the main paper.

The separate Three-State Architecture and Experimental Specification is its companion. The papers carry research edition v1.0 without editorial draft labels; covers are intentionally clean. ## Publication checks

Confirm final paper identity and title; complete substantive review; preserve supported claims and limitations. The standalone research approach original prose has its own adopted CC BY 4.0 scope. The historical controlled original paper, cover and helper grants remain in force; [the content scope](../RESEARCH_V1_CONTENT_LICENSE_SCOPE.json) distinguishes those grants from approved current identities and the separately recorded policy decision. Check retained and third-party notices separately. Existing report grants do not automatically license the new findings manuscript, architecture companion or all helper files.

Review the exact publication snapshot and every included asset. For an existing repository, examine all reachable Git history and branches, tags, releases, issues, PRs, workflow logs/artifacts, LFS objects and linked external storage. Replacing the current README or removing one release does not conceal earlier content. Preserve private records outside the public distribution. If a full public disclosure review cannot clear the existing history, prepare a clean public distribution while retaining a private canonical archive.

Verify manifests, checksums, links, licence notices, authorship and reading order. Verify public/private separation using a non-member account before sharing access. Review scientific claims and safety results separately from archive integrity. Publish only after the owner approves the exact hashes and destination.

## Later editions

Each public research edition provides a contribution route under its current guide while the next edition begins privately. For example, Research v1.0 supports contributions toward review for private research v2.0 Lab participation; a selected Research v2.0 edition may later become public, followed by private research v3.0. Use the [research-cycle guide](RESEARCH_CYCLE.md) for audience and access details.

Publication follows satisfactory review of the selected evidence, reproducibility, rights, safety and disclosure scope. Publish a curated public repository/release from a reviewed snapshot; keep private Lab history and separately controlled Strategic development private. Completion does not require publishing every file or opening the entire working repository.

Owners select what is published, deferred or withheld under the applicable terms. Strategic development is optional and parallel; it is not a required stage before publication. For contributed private material proposed for Strategic retention or a new Strategic use, record an asset-specific agreement with the contributor or applicable rights holder and the authorised project representative. Identify the permitted purpose, audience, duration and disclosure terms, together with any additional permissions required. Existing public permissions remain unchanged.

Reassess qualifying contributions, owner approval and participation scope for each private cycle; access does not carry forward automatically. Record which existing contributions were considered rather than implying two new merges are automatically required at every transition. Retain the [publication notice](PUBLICATION_NOTICE.md) in each edition.
